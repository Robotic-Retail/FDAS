# Fire Detection and Alarm System (FDAS) Requirement for Industrial Zone Buildings – Philippines (RA 9514)

## 1. General Rule

Under **RA 9514 (Fire Code of the Philippines)** and its **2019 RIRR**, an **approved automatic fire detection and alarm system** (which requires an FACP) is required for all industrial occupancies.

## 2. Exceptions (Manual Fire Alarm System Suffices)

| Exception | Section (2019 RIRR) | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| < 25 occupants | § 10.2.17.3, C(1) – Industrial Occupancies | Buildings with **less than 25 occupants** |
| ≤ 2,000 m² ordinary/low hazard | § 10.2.18.2, B(1) – Storage Occupancies | Buildings with **ordinary or low hazard** not exceeding **2,000 m²** |

- The two exceptions apply to **different occupancy classifications** and do **not** need to be met simultaneously.
- The BFP **Fire Safety Enforcement Manual (2013)** applies both provisions to industrial zones.

## 3. Manual Fire Alarm System

- Initiated **by a person** via a pull station / manual call point.
- Components: pull stations, audible/visual notification appliances, control panel (no detection loops), power supply.
- Placement: natural path of escape, near each exit, max **30 m** horizontal distance from any point on the same floor.

## 4. High Hazard Classification

**Definition (RIRR § 10.2.4.2):**
> "Those which are liable to burn with **extreme rapidity** or from which **poisonous gases or explosions** are to be expected in the event of fire."

**Typical high-hazard examples:**
- Bulk flammable liquid storage (Class I, II, IIIA)
- LPG storage
- High-piled combustible storage > 4 m (or > 2.4 m for highly combustibles)
- Combustible dust generation (sawmills, grain elevators)
- Organic peroxides, flammable solids, cellulose nitrate
- NFPA 231 Classes III–V

**If classified high hazard → automatic system (FACP) is required regardless of floor area or occupant count.**

## 5. Application: Cooking Oil Storage + Refilling Station

- Cooking oil is **Class IIIB combustible liquid** (flash point ~300–350 °C).
- Does **not** meet the high-hazard definition (burns slowly, no toxic gases, no explosion risk).
- Max allowable quantity for Class IIIB: **50,600 L** (well above typical refilling station volumes).
- **Conclusion:** A standard cooking oil bulk storage + decanting/refilling setup remains **ordinary/low hazard**. The exceptions apply, and a **manual fire alarm system** is the appropriate minimum requirement — no FACP needed.
